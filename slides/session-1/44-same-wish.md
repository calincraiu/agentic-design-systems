<!-- notes -->
**The idea.** You gave the same words twice today. At the start, Chat gave you one answer: fluent, confident, with numbers in it. Now your own system has given you a brief, three measured towers and a report on the trade-offs.

Compare them as a client would:

- **Numbers.** Where did each number come from? In Chat, it was written because it fitted. In your gallery, each number was measured by a tool, and you can rerun it and get the same result. But a tool only measures what its metrics cover: the tower's shadow figure is all the ground in shadow, not the park's. Neither answer measured the park.
- **Assumptions.** Did Chat say what it assumed about the park, the river, the plot? Your brief has a section for exactly that, and each limit is marked "you" or "assumed".
- **Choice.** Chat gave you one answer. Your system gave you three strategies and asked you to choose.

**Why it matters.** Neither answer tells you whether the tower is *good*. But only one of them lets you see how it was made and what it assumed, and that's what you need before you can argue about "good". That difference comes from the parts you added today: tools, a skill, context, and you in the loop.

**Try it yourself.** Find one thing the Chat answer said that you now know to be a guess. Then find one thing your gallery can't tell you either. That second one is your take-home for Session 2.
