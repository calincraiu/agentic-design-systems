# AI is a family. An LLM is the one that writes.

<div data-widget="figure" data-name="ai-family"></div>

> Which of these have you already used? Where does your favourite render tool sit?

<!-- notes -->
"AI" covers very different kinds of program, so a claim about AI means little until you know which kind. The diagram nests them:

- **Artificial intelligence** is any program that does something we'd call clever, from a chess engine to a route planner.
- **Machine learning** is the part of AI that learns from examples instead of following rules someone wrote. A spam filter learns from emails people have marked as spam.
- **Generative AI** is trained on a large collection of examples and then produces new things in the same vein.
- An **LLM** (large language model) is generative AI for text. Claude, GPT and Gemini are LLMs. Many can read images too, but what they produce is text.
- An **image model** is generative AI for pictures: Midjourney, Stable Diffusion and most AI render plugins.

The boundary is blurring, and some recent models handle both text and pictures. The diagram groups them by what each is mainly used for.

What training produces is called a **model**: a very large file of numbers, adjusted until it is good at producing one kind of output. A render model and an LLM work differently inside, but both produce what is likely given what they were trained on, not something that has been checked. This course is about the LLM and the system you build around it.
