# Eight towers, one tool

<div data-widget="contact" data-images="slim-beacon,quarter-turn,squat-block,leaning,flared-top,long-slab,full-turn,needle"></div>

> Which one is best? **How would you know?**
>
> And if it stands next to a park?

<!-- notes -->
**The idea.** In class, the words people reach for here are usually "elegant", "grounded" or "dramatic". Those are real qualities, but each one is an opinion until you can say *what you would look at* to check it. Is "grounded" a wide base? A low centre of mass? Little overhang? Each answer points to something you could measure.

Then the question changed: *next to a park*. Did your favourite change? "Best" only means something once you know what the tower is for. That is a **brief**. And "next to a park" points at something you could measure, such as how much shadow falls on the grass, if the tool knew where the park was.

All eight towers came from the same tool, given eight different sets of numbers: floors, size, twist, taper, lean and so on. Part 2 comes back to that tool. By then you'll see that what a system *can measure* decides what it can notice.

**Why it matters.** An agent can produce a hundred options in an afternoon. Choosing between them is the design work. Making the choice arguable, with words *and* numbers, is what this course is about.

**Try it yourself.** Pick your favourite of the eight and write one sentence on why. Then underline every word in it that a tool could check.
