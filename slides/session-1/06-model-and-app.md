# One model, many apps

<div data-widget="figure" data-name="model-app"></div>

> The **model** writes. The **app** decides what the model is given, and what it can reach.

<!-- notes -->
**The idea.** "Claude" is the name of three things at once:

- a **company's product name**
- a **model**: the file of learned numbers that reads text and writes text. It comes in versions and sizes (Opus, Sonnet, Haiku), and you can pick one in the app.
- an **app**: the program around the model. It shows you the conversation, adds its own instructions (which you don't see), gives the model tools, and asks you before it acts.

The Chat tab and the Code tab are two tabs of one desktop app, and each wraps **the same model** in different equipment: its own instructions, its own tools, its own permissions. In this course, "the app" means exactly that: the program around the model, and what it gives the model. Other companies work the same way: ChatGPT is OpenAI's app around OpenAI's GPT models.

In this course, "Claude" on its own means the whole thing you talk to: the model in its app. When the difference matters, the slides say "the model" or "the app".

**Why it matters.** Everything you author in this course lives on the app's side: your `CLAUDE.md`, your skills, your tools, your permissions. You never change the model. You change what's around it. That's also why what you learn here carries over to other apps, under other names. And models are updated over time: a tool gives the same answer next year, but a model may not.

**Try it yourself.** In Chat, ask the same question twice, choosing a different model in the picker each time. That's the same app with a different model. Later today, ask the same question in the Code tab: the same model, with different equipment.
