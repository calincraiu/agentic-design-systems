# Ask Chat for a tower

> In the Chat tab, start a new conversation and paste:
>
> **"Design a tower on a 30 × 30 m plot next to a park: calm, but noticed from the river. Give me the number of floors, the floor height, the total height, the floor area and the shadow it casts on the park."**

> Everyone on the same model, please: the one the app picks by default.
>
> Read the answer. Then **keep that conversation open**: you'll come back to it at the end of the session.

<!-- notes -->
This is how most people use AI: ask a question, get an answer back. You probably got a well-written reply with numbers in it, and perhaps a name for the tower. The course calls this the bottom step of the *ladder of authorship*, **prompting**. The ladder comes back on the last slide.

Read the answer as a client would. Where did each number come from? Was the floor area worked out from the floors and the plot, or does it just look plausible? What did it assume about the park, the river and the direction of the sun, and did it tell you? You can't tell from the answer alone, which is why you keep it. At the end of the session you give the same wish to a system you have built and compare the two.

Everyone uses the app's default model, with memory switched off if your account has it on. Three activities today pool the room's answers (this one, the long sum and the fairness poll), and pooling only works if everyone asked the same model the same thing.

Ask the same question again in a second new conversation and compare the numbers. If they differ, decide which you would believe, and on what grounds.
